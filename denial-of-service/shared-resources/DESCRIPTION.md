Renting a server in the cloud often means renting a share of a physical machine. Several customers may have separate virtual machines on the same host, and storage can be shared across many workloads. This keeps hosting costs down, but it also makes resource limits part of keeping tenants from affecting one another. If one tenant can consume too much of a shared resource, another tenant's application can slow down even though its own traffic has not changed. That is the [*noisy neighbor*](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/antipatterns/noisy-neighbor/noisy-neighbor) problem.

Here, your programs run alongside the victim service on the same system, sharing CPU time, memory, and storage. Direct traffic from your user to `victim.internal` is blocked, while the monitor is still allowed to reach `/health` and check whether the service is responding.

The earlier attacks made the service consume resources on your behalf. With local access, you can consume those resources directly from the challenge terminal. Make the victim unable to answer in time by putting pressure on what it needs to run, while leaving the monitor able to perform its checks and report the result.
